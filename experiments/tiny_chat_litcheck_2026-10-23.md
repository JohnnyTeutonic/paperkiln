# Tiny chat models with a multi-turn proxy benchmark: literature check (23 Oct 2026)

Idea under test: train 5M-150M models for basic multi-turn chat, scored by a fixed 5-turn exchange where a later turn needs a fact from an earlier one; compare shapes (shallow/wide vs deep/thin) and dialogue-shaped components across seeds. Every claim below was read from the primary source (arXiv abstract or HTML, ACL Anthology, official model config) on 23 Oct 2026.

## 2. Open vs taken (conservative)

TAKEN (close variants exist):
- Deep vs wide at 100M+ on loss and reasoning (MobileLLM, Gemstones, Petty, Kaplan).
- Dialogue-only or chat-only pretraining of small models (2510.20358, 2604.19642, MiniMind, TinyDialogues).
- Teacher-in-the-loop interaction training of small LMs (BabyLM 2025, ContingentChat).
- Role/segment embeddings in decoders (ISE, ASIDE); turn-level hierarchy and relative turn attention (HRED, DialogBERT, 2011.08067, 2110.06823); segment-counting positions (CoPE); memory/compression tokens (RMT, gist).
- Multi-turn carry-over probes for large models (MT-Eval Recollection, MultiChallenge, MT-Bench-101).

OPEN (nothing found after targeted searches):
- Depth vs width measured on multi-turn context carry-over at 5M-150M, matched parameters and tokens, multi-seed. Closest: μLMs (single-turn), TinyStories (story consistency).
- A fixed, template-generated, log-prob-scorable 5-turn carry-over benchmark for tiny models with distance and distractor controls.
- Any of the TAKEN dialogue mechanisms (role embeddings, turn biases, per-turn state tokens) trained from scratch at tiny scale and judged on carry-over rather than safety or perplexity. The contribution would be the measurement and the scale, not the mechanism; it must be framed that way.

Expected conflict: the literature predicts depth helps exactly the ability the benchmark measures (TinyStories on context tracking; MobileLLM; Gemstones per FLOP). A shallow/wide win on carry-over would contradict three sources, so it needs seeds, matched FLOPs and the MQAR control before it is believed. A depth win would replicate MobileLLM on a new metric, which is still a usable result for picking the next base's shape.

## 3. The proxy benchmark (shared by every experiment)

"Carry-5": templated 5-turn dialogues (user/assistant), entities and values drawn from held-out pools so nothing can be memorised from training. Fact planted at turn 1, 2 or 3; queried at turn 4 or 5 (distance control, after MT-Eval); 0-2 distractor facts of the same type (MultiChallenge-style interference); single-turn twin of every item (fact and question in one turn) to separate carry-over from basic QA. Scoring by forced choice over candidate values (log-prob, BabyLM minimal-pair style) plus greedy exact match, so a 5M model gets a non-trivial signal. Decontaminate against lever 1 and lever 2 corpora with `corpus/decontam.py`. Report the carry-over gap (single-turn minus multi-turn accuracy) as the headline, by distance.

## 4. Experiments (Colab, cheap, 3-5 seeds each, paired by seed)

**E1. Shape sweep on carry-over.** At ~20M and ~60M non-embedding parameters, 4 shapes each (e.g. L4, L8, L16, L30 at matched parameters), same tokens and lever-2 mix, then lever-1 SFT. Report Carry-5, the single-turn twin, val loss, MQAR recall and wall-clock. Must distinguish from MobileLLM and Gemstones (reasoning/loss, 125M+), μLMs (single-turn, two shapes) and TinyStories (stories). Pre-register the direction: depth predicted to win on carry-over.

**E2. Role embeddings from scratch.** Baseline: chat-template tokens only (what everyone does). Arms: ISE-style learned role embedding added to token embeddings; ASIDE-style rotation for assistant tokens; both at the best E1 shape. Cost is a few thousand parameters. Must beat the template-only baseline on Carry-5 and must not lose single-turn accuracy; distinguish from ISE/ASIDE by scale, from-scratch training and the carry-over metric.

**E3. Turn-index position.** Add a second positional signal on turn index (a turn-RoPE component or an ALiBi-like per-head bias on turn distance) vs CoPE gated on turn-boundary tokens vs plain RoPE. Prediction from MT-Eval: gains, if any, grow with distance. Must beat CoPE (the general learned-counting version) and be framed against 2110.06823 and 2011.08067 (turn-level relative attention in encoders).

**E4. Per-turn state tokens.** k write tokens at the end of each turn; later turns see only the state tokens plus the current turn (gist-style mask), vs full attention (upper bound) vs no history (lower bound). Measures how much of Carry-5 survives compression, and shrinks the KV cache for ember. Must be distinguished from RMT and gisting: the novelty is per-turn dialogue state at tiny scale, not the mechanism.

**E5. The data bar.** Same shape, dialogue share in pretraining 0% / 27% / 47% / 100% (lever-2 ratios), lever-1 SFT. TurnWise and 2510.20358 show data moves multi-turn ability; E2-E4 gains should be reported against the gap this sweep opens, so an architecture win is not a disguised data effect. Run first or alongside E1; it also tests whether the next base's 27%/47% split is sensible.

Order: E5 and E1 together (they share the pipeline and set the shape), then E2 and E3 (cheap deltas), then E4. Keep the same tokeniser (16k Llama 3 scheme) so winners export to ember.
