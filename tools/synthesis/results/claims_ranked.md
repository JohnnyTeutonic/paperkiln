## Evidence, per claim

### rope-better-learned: RoPE beats learned and sinusoidal absolute positions in-distribution, not just for extrapolation

Verdict TESTED-WEAK; our prior: RoFormer: MT and GLUE with few seeds; most later evidence is about extrapolation

- [None] Rotary Embeddings: A Relative Revolution (EleutherAI blog, Biderman et al. 2021): "125M GPT-NeoX on OpenWebText2, 55k steps: Learned Absolute loss 2.809, T5 RPE 2.801, Rotary 2.759; 1.4B on the Pile, 8k steps: Learned 2.240, T5 2.223, Rotary 2.173; seeds not stated, hyperparameters copied from GPT-3 sizes without per-arm tuning." (The most-cited in-distribution evidence: a ~0.05-0.07 nat RoPE win over learned at 125M and 1.4B, single run each, no sinusoidal arm.)
- [None] Train Short, Test Long: Attention with Linear Biases (Press et al., ICLR 2022): "247M WikiText-103 model evaluated at its training length L=1024: Sinusoidal 19.34, Rotary 19.33, T5 bias 18.80, ALiBi 18.66 perplexity; the sinusoidal baseline has std 0.24 across seeds (count not stated)." (In-distribution, RoPE is indistinguishable from sinusoidal at 247M; relative-bias methods beat both.)
- [None] What Language Model to Train if You Have One Million GPU Hours? (Le Scao et al., 2022): "1.3B, 112B tokens, single run per arm, EAI-harness average: ALiBi 43.70, Learned 41.71, Rotary 41.46, None 41.23; ALiBi's lead held "even without taking into account their ability to extrapolate"." (The one contrary datapoint: learned >= rotary on downstream tasks at 1.3B, single run, noisy metric.)
- [None] nanogpt-from-scratch paired-seed ablation study (unrefereed GitHub report): "51.2M params, 3 seeds, 524M tokens, T=1024: pos-rope -0.0886 +/- 0.0021 val loss vs learned absolute (seed noise floor 0.0043), all seeds agreed; pos-none +0.0928 +/- 0.0110." (Nano-scale, matched-budget, 3-seed evidence that RoPE beats learned in-distribution by ~20x the noise floor; no sinusoidal arm.)
- Proposed test: learned vs sinusoidal vs rope at fixed T, matched params, 12 seeds, in-distribution loss only (knobs: position)

### window-loses-longrange: Sliding-window attention loses long-range dependencies that full attention captures, at every scale

Verdict TESTED-WEAK; our prior: measured on long-context benchmarks at scale; at nano scale the transfer study found the window lane LEADS early and trails late, with the crossing moving with width

- [None] SWAT: Sliding Window Attention Training for Efficient Large Language Models (Fu et al., 2025): "340M/15B-token and 760M/30B-token models from scratch; within training length (T=1024) sliding-window 3.02 vs full-attention 2.97 perplexity on OpenWebText, while at 16,384 tokens the window model holds 3.005 against full attention's 4.84; single run per arm." (The only from-scratch matched-budget window-vs-full comparison at sub-1B scale: a ~1.7% in-distribution penalty for the window, single-seed, no training-curve or width analysis.)
- [None] Gemma 3 Technical Report (Gemma Team, 2025): "2B text-only ablations: local:global ratios 1:1 to 7:1 - "The impact is minimal, even with 7-to-1 local to global"; window sizes 512-4096 at 1:1 and 1:3 - "the sliding window can be reduced significantly without impacting perplexity"; no long-context (RULER/NIAH) isolation of window size; single runs." (At 2B with some global layers, perplexity is insensitive to window size; the loss the claim predicts does not show up in perplexity, only (untested here) on long-range evals.)
- [None] Simple Local Attentions Remain Competitive for Long-Context Tasks (Xiong et al., NAACL 2022): "RoBERTa-large variants pretrained "using the same long-doc corpus" (100k updates, 2^20 tokens per batch) then fine-tuned; "none of the tested efficient attentions can beat a simple local window attention"; MLM perplexity local window 3.47 vs blockwise 3.39 vs long-short 3.36; Hyperpartisan averaged over 4 seeds, other tasks single run." (Controlled pretraining shows local attention is not worse than fancier sparse patterns on long-doc tasks; it does not include a same-length full-attention arm, so it is silent on the claim's comparison.)
- [None] The Sparse Frontier: Sparse Attention Trade-offs in Transformer LLMs (Nawrot et al., 2025): ""the largest-scale empirical analysis to date of training-free sparse attention, evaluating six methods across multiple model families and sizes, sequences up to 128K tokens, and sparsity levels up to 0.95" on nine tasks; local/window patterns degrade on long-range retrieval and reasoning tasks; "longer sequences tolerate higher sparsity"." (The long-range loss is measured post hoc on 7B-72B models, not trained in; it establishes the at-scale half of the claim only.)
- Proposed test: window vs exact at matched budget across T and width, milestone-matched, 12 seeds; where is the crossing and does it exist at T=256 (knobs: attention)

### lr-scales-mup: The optimal learning rate falls with width as 1/width under standard parameterisation, and muP makes it constant

Verdict CONTESTED; our prior: muP paper: shown on transformers to GPT-3 scale; at nano scale the transfer study found lr* 2.5e-4, 5e-4, 1.25e-4 for widths 256, 512, 1024 under SP, which is NOT monotone

- [None] Tensor Programs V: Tuning Large Neural Networks via Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer (Yang et al.): "We show that, in the recently discovered Maximal Update Parametrization (muP), many optimal HPs remain stable even as model size changes. ... by transferring pretraining HPs from a model of 13M parameters, we outperform published numbers of BERT-large (350M parameters) ... by transferring from 40M parameters, we outperform published numbers of the 6.7B GPT-3 model." (muP LR transfer demonstrated on transformers at two scales; the SP-optimum-falls-with-width claim is shown in sweep figures, not with seeds; no seed counts reported.)
- [None] An Empirical Study of muP Learning Rate Transfer (Lingle): "Studying over a dozen ablations with up to 1.2B parameters and 33B tokens and a large-scale experiment with up to 10B parameters and 190B tokens, we observe a positive answer for most settings, and discuss improvements otherwise. ... for all model sizes, the optimal loss in the sweep is higher [for standard parameterization] than the optimal loss for the baseline muP models. ... The same seed is used for all experiments." (Independent replication: muP LR transfer holds for most settings up to 10B; failures tied to trainable gains, bf16 attention scale and unembedding init; single seed.)
- [None] Scaling Exponents Across Parameterizations and Optimizers (Everett et al.): "Our results show that all parameterizations, not just maximal update parameterization (muP), can achieve hyperparameter transfer; moreover, our novel per-layer learning rate prescription for standard parameterization outperforms muP." (14 sizes up to 26.8B, tens of thousands of runs: SP with per-layer LR transfers too and beats muP; the global-LR SP optimum drifts with width but not as the naive 1/width; no seeds reported.)
- [None] On the Surprising Effectiveness of Large Learning Rates under Standard Width Scaling (Haas et al., NeurIPS 2025): "In practice, however, optimal learning rates decay slower than theoretically predicted and networks exhibit both stable training and non-trivial feature learning, even at very large widths. ... width-scaling considerations are surprisingly useful for predicting empirically maximal stable learning rate exponents which provide useful guidance on optimal learning rate exponents." (Pythia-GPT up to width 4096 / 1.4B under SP: the optimal LR falls with width but slower than 1/width (controlled-divergence regime under CE loss); seeds not stated.)
- Proposed test: lr sweep per width under SP, 12 seeds, matched tokens; is lr*(width) monotone at nano scale (knobs: d, lr)

### sparse-transfer: Architectural conclusions drawn at small scale transfer to large scale

Verdict CONTESTED; our prior: transfer_s2 (Sep 2026): sign structure of a sparse-attention panel did NOT transfer from d=256 to d=512 under per-width tuning; agreement decays with position on the loss curve

- [None] Scaling Laws vs Model Architectures: How does Inductive Bias Influence Scaling? (Tay et al.; EMNLP Findings 2023): "Systematic study of scaling behaviour of ten diverse model architectures (Transformers, Switch Transformers, Universal Transformers, Dynamic convolutions, Performers, MLP-Mixers) ... the best performing model can fluctuate at different scales." (Direct controlled evidence that architecture rankings do not hold across scale (single runs per point).)
- [None] Rethinking Conventional Wisdom in Machine Learning: From Generalization to Scaling (Xiao): "We identify a new phenomenon termed 'scaling law crossover', where two scaling curves intersect at a certain scale, implying that methods effective at smaller scales may not generalize to larger ones ... explicit L2 regularization and implicit regularization through small batch sizes and large learning rates may no longer hold." (Regularisation-type knobs (which include sparsity) are the class most prone to small-to-large sign reversal; raises the 'single experiment at scale' comparison problem.)
- [None] Small-scale proxies for large-scale Transformer training instabilities (Wortsman et al.; ICLR 2024): "Instabilities also appear in small models when training at high learning rates, and mitigations previously employed at large scales are equally effective in this regime ... a 4.8B model trained at the predicted learning rate diverged as predicted." (The pro-transfer side: stability mechanisms and their mitigations (qk-layernorm, z-loss, warmup, weight decay, muP) transfer from small proxies, with a confirmed extrapolation.)
- [None] Small-to-Large Generalization: Data Influences Models Consistently Across Scale (Khaddaj, Engstrom, Madry): "Changes in data do not influence smaller and larger models identically ... we find that small- and large-scale language model predictions (generally) do highly correlate across choice of training data." (Data-side choices transfer well from proxies; supports the claim for the data axis, not the architecture axis.)
- Proposed test: the finding exists; the claim-level test is whether other knobs (norm, activation, position) transfer across width under the same protocol (knobs: norm, activation, position, d)

### relu-dead-llm: ReLU MLPs are strictly worse than smooth activations for language models

Verdict CONTESTED; our prior: no controlled multi-seed comparison at matched budget in wide circulation; ReLU revival papers (sparsity) report parity

- [None] GLU Variants Improve Transformer (Shazeer 2020): "Four-run variance at 65k steps: FFN-ReLU 1.997 (0.005) vs FFN-GELU 1.983 (0.005) vs FFN-Swish 1.994 (0.003); at 524k steps ReLU 1.677 vs GELU 1.679 vs Swish 1.683 (matched d_ff=3072)." (ReLU vs GELU at full budget is a dead heat (ReLU marginally lower); at 1/8 budget GELU leads by ~2-3 std.)
- [None] Do Transformer Modifications Transfer Across Implementations and Applications? (Narang et al., EMNLP 2021): "5 runs at 1/8 budget: Vanilla (ReLU) 2.182+/-0.005 vs GeLU 2.179+/-0.003; final loss 1.838 vs 1.838; SGLUE 71.66 vs 75.79 (single fine-tune runs)." (Pretraining loss identical within noise at 223M; the only GELU advantage is on single-run downstream fine-tuning.)
- [None] ReLU Strikes Back: Exploiting Activation Sparsity in Large Language Models (Mirzadeh et al., ICLR 2024): "OPT-1.3B trained from scratch "on a hundred billion tokens of the RefinedWeb datasets with different activation functions, including ReLU, SiLU, and GELU"; "the performance of the models is very similar when using different activation functions"; no seeds or variance reported." (Single-run 1.3B decoder parity of ReLU with GELU/SiLU on zero-shot benchmarks, plus ~90% activation sparsity for ReLU.)
- [None] ReLU^2 Wins: Discovering Efficient Activation Functions for Sparse LLMs (Zhang et al., 2024): "Four 1.3B LMs pretrained on 100B tokens: SwiGLU 50.53, ReGLU 50.36, ReLU^2 50.48, ReLU 48.85 average accuracy; "ReLU consistently underperforms" the GLU and squared variants; single run each." ### deeper-beats-wider: At fixed parameters, deeper models beat wider ones for language modelling

- [None] The Depth Delusion: Why Transformers Should Be Wider, Not Deeper (Fahim, Karim): "validated across 30 transformer architectures spanning 17M to 7B parameters ...
